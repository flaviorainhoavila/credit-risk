# Design: bust-out detection on consortium data (Part 2)

Status: draft for review, 2026-11-08. Nothing in this document has been built or run.

## Problem

Bust-out (first-party) fraud: a customer opens credit cards, builds a normal usage and repayment record, then runs balances up to the limits across cards and stops paying, with no intention of repaying. Because the pattern spans several lenders, one issuer sees only part of it. Data that combines a customer's accounts across institutions ("wallet" or consortium data) shows the whole pattern. Credit bureau data has the same advantage, as described in public patents on bust-out detection [1].

The model question: for an active cardholder at month t, what is the probability that the customer busts out (charges off across cards after a balance ramp) within the next H months? A useful model gives enough lead time to act (for example freeze line increases or review the account) and separates bust-out from genuine financial hardship, which also ends in charge-off but needs a different response.

## Why simulated data

No public dataset has bust-out labels or account data from several card issuers for the same customers. The plan has two data sources:

1. **A simulated consortium**, generated by code in this repo, where bust-out behavior is injected with known timing. It exercises the full data platform and gives exact labels. Metrics on it measure whether the pipeline and model recover a designed pattern, not real-world performance, and every result table must say so.
2. **Home Credit Default Risk** (Kaggle competition). `bureau.csv` and `bureau_balance.csv` hold each applicant's credits at other institutions with monthly status, the closest public analog to multi-institution data. The label is default, not bust-out. It is used to check simulator distributions and as a real-label benchmark for cross-institution features. The competition terms must be checked and accepted before download (pending).

Simulator parameters come only from public sources (patents, industry articles, published statistics), cited next to each parameter in the config. No employer features, data, thresholds or results are used.

## Simulator

Entities:

- `persons`: synthetic people with synthetic identifiers (name, date of birth, a national-id-like number) used only for linkage tests.
- `issuers`: 4 or 5 synthetic card issuers, each with its own feed format, schedule and quirks.
- `accounts`: cards held by persons at issuers, opened at different dates.
- `statements`: one row per account per month: credit limit, balance, payment, cash advance amount, days past due, status.

Behavior types (shares and parameters in config):

- **Normal**: stable utilization and payments, occasional late payment.
- **Hardship default**: an income shock, rising utilization over many months, partial payments, then delinquency and charge-off. The main confounder.
- **Bust-out**: a quiet period of normal behavior and on-time payments; credit line increases and new accounts at other issuers; then a fast ramp of balances and cash advances across cards, often with payments that later fail; then charge-off across issuers at about the same time.

Feed faults injected on purpose: late files, a schema change at one issuer, duplicate records, a broken month. These drive the data contracts, quality gates and alerts.

## Data platform

- **Lake (landing and raw)**: each issuer drops files in its own format (CSV, JSON lines, Parquet) to its prefix in MinIO, partitioned by month. Files are kept as received.
- **Data contracts**: one contract per issuer (schema, types, allowed values, freshness). A failing contract quarantines the file and raises an alert naming the contributor.
- **Linkage**: each issuer sends a keyed hash (HMAC) of normalized identifiers instead of raw identity. The warehouse links accounts to one consortium customer through the tokens. Linkage quality is measured against the simulator's ground truth.
- **Warehouse (dbt)**: staging per issuer, a common `core.statements` and `core.accounts`, and `core.customers` from linkage.
- **Marts**: `features` (one row per customer per month, cross-issuer wallet features), `ml` (labels, splits, scores), and a fraud operations mart for the Tableau dashboard (alerts by month, issuer and score band).
- **Streaming**: monthly statement updates published to Kafka-compatible topics (Redpanda); a consumer updates online features and rescores the customer.

## Label

A customer is a bust-out case at month t if the simulator assigned the bust-out path and charge-off falls in t+1 to t+H. The label for training is built only from observed fields (charge-off across issuers after a utilization ramp, no payments after the ramp), and the simulator truth is used to measure how often that observable proxy is wrong. Intent is not observable in real data, so the proxy and its error rate are part of the result.

## Features (examples, built in SQL)

Batch features use a 12-month lookback window ending at the scoring month. Bust-out behavior shifts over time, so the model is retrained on a rolling window and promoted only if it beats the current model on the latest months (same mechanism as Part 1, M5).

- Number of open accounts and issuers, new accounts in the last 3 and 6 months.
- Total and per-issuer utilization, and its change over 1, 3 and 6 months.
- Credit line increases across issuers in the last 6 months.
- Cash advance share of balance; payment-to-balance ratio; failed payments.
- Spread of behavior across issuers (for example utilization rising at all issuers at once).

## Real-time detection as a signal processing problem

Each customer's activity across issuers is a set of irregularly sampled time series (balances, payments, cash advances, line changes). A bust-out is a change in the generating process: a stable regime followed by a ramp. The streaming path treats it that way:

- **Recursive features with constant state**: exponentially weighted means and variances (first-order IIR filters) updated per event, instead of recomputing 12-month windows. The batch and streaming versions are tested for agreement.
- **Online change-point detection**: CUSUM on utilization and payment ratios, and Bayesian online change-point detection (Adams and MacKay, 2007 [4]), which gives a posterior probability that the customer's regime has changed.
- **State-space model**: a Kalman filter on a latent spending-ramp state per customer, combining observations from all issuers, with irregular sampling handled through the time step.
- **Score fusion**: the batch model's monthly score combined with the streaming change signals; evaluated by lead time gained over the batch model alone.

This needs finer data than monthly statements near the bust, so the simulator emits daily transactions in addition to monthly statements.

## Evaluation

- Out of time by month: train on early months, validate, test on later months.
- Precision and recall at a fixed review budget (for example the top 0.5% of customers per month).
- Lead time: months between the first alert and charge-off.
- Confusion with hardship defaults, reported separately.
- Comparison of single-issuer features (what one bank sees) against cross-issuer features (what the consortium sees). This is the central result of Part 2.
- Home Credit: the same comparison of within-lender against bureau features on a real default label.

## Privacy and governance

- No real personal data anywhere. Synthetic identifiers exist only to exercise tokenized linkage.
- Notes on consent-based data sharing (Open Finance Brasil), LGPD (purpose limitation, minimization) and adverse-action explanations for fraud decisions.
